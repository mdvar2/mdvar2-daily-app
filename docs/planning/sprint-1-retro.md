# CareerLane Sprint 1 Retrospective

## What Went Well

Breaking the Sprint 1 backlog items into full user stories and writing acceptance criteria made the work much clearer. Some items that looked simple as short backlog phrases turned out to contain more work than I first expected. Using Story Points also helped me compare the size of the stories. The board-related stories were smaller, while adding and displaying job applications required more work and received higher estimates. The Status field made it easier to see what I had started and what was still waiting. It also helped me avoid treating every Sprint 1 item as if it was already being worked on.

## What Did Not Go Well

My original Sprint 1 only contained three backlog items, which was too small for the requirements of this sprint exercise. I had to review the backlog and bring two Application Tracking items into Sprint 1. I also realised that some of my original backlog phrases did not explain enough about the expected behaviour. For example, "Add a new job application card to a board" sounded simple until I wrote the acceptance criteria and identified that the application needs information, must be saved to the correct board and needs an initial application stage. Another issue is that the Sprint Goal has not yet been fully achieved. Two stories are currently In Progress and three remain To Do, so there is still work to complete before the full CareerLane workflow can be demonstrated.

## Action for Sprint 2

Before moving any story into Sprint 2, I will write its full user story and acceptance criteria first and check that it meets my Definition of Ready. I will also estimate it before committing it to the sprint. This should help me identify hidden work earlier and avoid bringing unclear or oversized items into a sprint.
